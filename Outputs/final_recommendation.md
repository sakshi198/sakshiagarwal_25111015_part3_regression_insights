# Task 9: Final Recommendation

## Final Business Recommendation

Based on the regression analysis, the factors most strongly associated with monthly sales are:

* **Footfall**
* **Inventory Availability Percentage**
* **Marketing Spend**
* **Customer Rating**

These variables showed statistically significant relationships with monthly sales and had strong positive coefficients in the final multiple regression model.

The final model achieved an **R-squared of 0.81**, meaning it explains **81% of the variation in monthly sales**, making it the strongest model among all tested.

---

## Which Factors Are Most Important?

### 1. Footfall

Footfall appears to be the strongest driver of sales.

The coefficient of **33.782** shows that every additional customer visit increases monthly sales significantly.

This suggests that attracting more customers into stores should be a top priority.

---

### 2. Inventory Availability

Inventory availability had a strong positive effect.

The coefficient of **2991.993** shows that stores with better stock levels perform much better.

Leadership should focus on reducing stockouts and improving supply chain efficiency.

---

### 3. Marketing Spend

Marketing spend also showed a positive relationship.

The coefficient of **1.194** indicates that increasing marketing investment improves sales.

This supports continued investment in targeted promotions.

---

### 4. Customer Rating

Customer satisfaction matters.

A higher customer rating was associated with significantly higher sales.

This shows the importance of improving customer experience.

---

## Variables Leadership Should Focus On

Leadership should prioritize:

* Increasing store traffic through promotions and better customer acquisition
* Maintaining high inventory availability
* Improving customer experience and service quality
* Optimizing marketing budgets for maximum return

These areas showed the strongest statistical relationship with sales.

---

## Variables That Should Not Be Over-Interpreted

The dummy variable **region_north** had a p-value of **0.667**, which means it was not statistically significant.

This suggests regional location alone may not strongly impact sales.

Leadership should avoid making major regional decisions based only on this model.

---

## Recommended Business Actions

Based on the regression results, the company should:

1. Increase marketing in stores with high conversion potential.
2. Improve inventory management to prevent stock shortages.
3. Launch initiatives to increase footfall, such as promotions and events.
4. Improve customer service and store experience to raise customer ratings.
5. Monitor underperforming stores more closely.

These actions are directly supported by the strongest regression variables.

---

## Risks and Limitations

Although the model is strong, there are limitations:

* The model does not include seasonality.
* Competitor actions may affect store sales.
* Special promotions were not directly measured.
* Local economic conditions may impact store performance.
* Some high residual stores show that hidden variables still exist.

This means the model should support decisions, not replace business judgment.

---

## Association vs Causation

Regression shows **association**, not proof of causation.

For example:

Higher marketing spend is associated with higher sales, but this does not automatically mean marketing alone caused the increase.

Other hidden factors may also influence sales.

To prove causation, the company would need controlled experiments or A/B testing.

Regression helps identify important patterns, but business decisions should combine statistical evidence with operational knowledge.

---

## Final Conclusion

The multiple regression model should be used as the primary decision-support model.

It provides the most complete view of sales performance and identifies the most important areas leadership can improve.

The strongest focus should be on:

* customer traffic
* stock availability
* customer satisfaction
* effective marketing

Improving these factors is most likely to improve monthly sales performance.
